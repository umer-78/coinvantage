How the AI learns and how it is tested

Every call is graded against what price did next, in the cloud, whether or not a page is open. A method only survives if it keeps working after the market shifts.

The learning loop

  1. Collect live market data. Price, volume, volatility, momentum, and other market features are captured from the public exchange streams and API feeds.
  2. Score the signal. Each candidate signal, indicator, or forecast is evaluated using the actual market move that followed.
  3. Update what is trusted. The system keeps the stronger patterns and rejects the ones that fail in real conditions.
  4. Repeat on a rolling basis. The test window moves forward, so the model is always measured against fresh data rather than a single historical snapshot.

Why the testing matters

Walk-forward testing is important because it reduces hindsight bias. A model may look strong on a static backtest, but if it falls apart when the market regime changes, it is not useful.

CoinVantage evaluates every prediction against what happened next. That means the AI trader, the forecast overlay, and the signal logic all have to prove themselves in ongoing conditions, not just in a polished demo.

What gets measured

  • Directional accuracy of the forecast
  • Signal quality versus subsequent price movement
  • Whether an indicator adds value beyond noise, randomness, and trend-following alone
  • Whether the model still performs across multiple market conditions

How to read the results

The goal is not to claim certainty. The goal is to show the evidence: what was predicted, what happened next, and whether the model improved with each round of learning.

Market behavior changes over time. A good forecast system is transparent, measured, and adaptive. If a strategy starts losing edge, it is either adjusted or shown clearly as weaker than before.
